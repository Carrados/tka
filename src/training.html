Up to five hours of free one-to-one training, shaped around what you need to do.

Technology for Work

For members getting ready for paid work, volunteering or work experience. It helps you gain the IT skills needed in the workplace so that, with other pre-employment support, you are ready to take the step.

  1. An assessment. We find out the kind of work you are looking for and the IT skills you already have, and agree a learning plan.
  2. Setting up. We suggest the software or hardware likely to help most. We can connect to your laptop or PC remotely to install and configure it, and tell you about trusted suppliers if you need to buy anything.
  3. Five one-hour sessions, usually a week apart, to build skills and confidence, with tasks between sessions if they help.
  4. A follow-up a few weeks later, for anything you still need help with, and our recommendations for learning on your own and for equipment that might help at work.

Depending on your goals, it can cover the access features built into Windows and Microsoft Office, screen readers such as JAWS or NVDA, Microsoft Office, Zoom and Microsoft Teams, web browsing, and Apple and Android phones and their apps. It is normally delivered online, on Zoom.

Who it suits

We are flexible, but you are likely to get the most from this course if you:

  • have already had some pre-employment support or careers advice, such as help with your CV, interviews or job searching
  • have a laptop or PC running Windows 10 or 11
  • have good keyboard skills, especially for learning a screen reader (if not, we can suggest exercises to build them first)
  • are 18 or over
  • are a member: joining is free

If that isn't you yet but you are keen to improve your skills, get in touch anyway, and we will see what advice or support we can offer.

Technology for Learning

For visually impaired students aged 18 and over, to build the skills you need for education and to prepare for the world of work.

Training can cover screen readers or magnification with Microsoft Outlook, Excel, Word and PowerPoint, Zoom and Teams, web browsers, and smartphones and their apps. We shape it around the skills you are likely to need, give you resources to keep learning afterwards, advise on equipment and software, can set up your PC remotely, and can tell you about grant-making trusts that may help pay for technology.
